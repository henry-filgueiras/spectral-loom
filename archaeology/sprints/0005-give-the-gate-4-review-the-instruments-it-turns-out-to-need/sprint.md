# Give the gate 4 review the instruments it turns out to need

## Goal

Build whatever the gate 4 review turns out to need, while the review is happening, without deciding
the review's outcome.

This sprint is **open-ended on purpose** and closes when Henry says so. Sprints 1-4 each had a
shape known in advance; this one does not, because its work is generated by a human looking at
evidence and finding that he cannot see something he needs to see. A sprint that had to be planned
first would either have to guess what those things are or would delay them until after the verdict —
and a verdict reached without the instrument that would have changed it is not worth much.

```text
Henry examines a claim
    → he can hear something the page cannot show him
    → the instrument gains the smallest thing that shows it
    → he examines the next claim
```

## Rationale

Sprint 4 built the Timeline Observatory to make the honest answer cheaper than the lazy one, and
within minutes of real use it was found to have a hole: **a rejected candidate is invisible.** The
page reads `song.timeline.json`, the timeline contains accepted onsets only, and so a person can
hear that something was missed but cannot click the thing that was missed.

For judging a *detector* that is a serious gap. An onset the rule rejected by 5.23 out of 74.65 is a
different fact about the rule than one it rejected by 21.22, and neither is visible at all. The
first real finding of the review — a hammer-on at 2.833 s on `bass` whose novelty was 28.41 against
a threshold of 42.17 — was only reachable by dropping out of the instrument and into a Python
shell. That is exactly the friction the Observatory exists to remove.

The rule this sprint works under is the same one sprint 4 stopped for: **the instruments may
improve, the algorithm may not.** Retuning a threshold because one instance was missed is fitting to
one specimen, which `decision:11` and `dragon:4` are both written against. Making the miss *visible*
is not the same act, and it is the one that lets a verdict be reached on evidence.

## Success criteria

- Every addition helps a person *see* or *hear* something that is already true, and none of them
  changes what the compiler produces. The compiled timeline's sha256 does not move.
- Anything recomputed for review is unmistakably marked as not being in the timeline, and is
  computed by the same code the compiler used rather than a second implementation of it.
- The review surface stays read-only: no writes to the timeline, its cache, or any review state.
- Loopback only, no external anything, one shared transport clock.
- The hermetic suite stays hermetic, the default environment stays light, and nothing generated
  becomes tracked.
- Observations from the review are recorded as they arrive, faithfully, without being resolved into
  a verdict that Henry has not given.

## Non-goals

- Reaching the gate 4 verdict. Henry reaches it; this sprint hands him what he needs to.
- Changing the activity rule, the onset rule, or any threshold. If the review concludes they are
  wrong, that is a decision, and it is written down before any code moves.
- Basic Pitch, `note` events, pitch or chroma inference. A hammer-on being a pitch change is exactly
  the pressure toward gate 5, and it is not yet gate 5.
- The gate 6 analytical projection. This is still one page for one gate.
- A second specimen or a corpus.
- Pushing commits.
